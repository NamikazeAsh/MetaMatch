# General Singles Concepts

These hold in every singles format, SV and Champions alike.

## Pivoting and Momentum
Pivoting means switching out while still doing something useful. Pivot moves (U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn, Parting Shot, Teleport) attack or debuff and then switch, so you see the opponent's choice before your next Pokemon comes in. Momentum is having the free switch: when your opponent has to react to you, you pick the matchup. A slow pivot is often better than a fast one, because the Pokemon that comes in after it enters safely after the opponent has already attacked. Pivoting into a Pokemon with Rocky Helmet or Iron Barbs, or into a Ghost type when using Rapid Spin, gives the momentum back.

## Speed Control
Speed decides who acts first, so teams plan how to move before their threats. The tools are Choice Scarf users that outspeed and revenge kill, priority moves that ignore Speed, boosting moves like Dragon Dance and Quiver Dance, Speed lowering moves and Sticky Web, and Trick Room, which reverses the order for five turns. When two Pokemon have the same Speed, the order is random each turn (a speed tie). Paralysis halves Speed.

## Priority Moves
Priority moves act before normal moves regardless of Speed: Extreme Speed (+2), Sucker Punch, Aqua Jet, Ice Shard, Bullet Punch, Mach Punch, Shadow Sneak and Quick Attack (+1), plus Grassy Glide in Grassy Terrain. Sucker Punch only works if the target is using an attacking move, so a status move or switch makes it fail. Psychic Terrain blocks priority against grounded Pokemon, and abilities like Armor Tail and Queenly Majesty block it too. Priority is the usual way to finish off faster threats that are already damaged.

## Entry Hazards
Stealth Rock damages every Pokemon that switches in, scaled by its weakness to Rock (from 3% to 50% of max HP). Spikes (up to three layers) and Toxic Spikes only hit grounded Pokemon. Sticky Web lowers the Speed of grounded Pokemon on entry. Hazards are removed by Rapid Spin, Defog, Mortal Spin and Tidy Up, and Heavy-Duty Boots make the holder ignore them. Ghost types can block Rapid Spin, and Magic Bounce reflects hazards back.

## Setup Sweepers and Win Conditions
A win condition is the plan that actually takes the last Pokemon down, often a setup sweeper that boosts with Swords Dance, Dragon Dance, Nasty Plot or Calm Mind and then attacks. Sweepers need their checks removed or weakened first: chip damage, hazards and trading away the Pokemon that stop them. Unaware ignores the opponent's boosts, Haze and Clear Smog reset them, and phazing moves like Whirlwind, Roar and Dragon Tail force the boosted Pokemon out.

## Checks and Counters
A counter can switch into a threat safely and then beat it one on one. A check beats it only in some situations, for example when coming in for free after a faint, or by outspeeding it. Revenge killing means bringing a faster Pokemon or priority user in after something faints to knock out a damaged threat. Good teams have at least one counter or two checks for every major threat in the format.

## Double Switching and Prediction
A double switch is switching when you expect the opponent to switch too, so your new Pokemon lands on the Pokemon they bring in. It wins big when right and loses momentum when wrong. Safer plays, like attacking with a move that hits both likely switch ins, are usually better unless you know the opponent's habits.
