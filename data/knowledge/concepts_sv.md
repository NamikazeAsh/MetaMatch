# Scarlet and Violet Concepts

SV singles (OU, UU, National Dex). Examples use Pokemon from the SV tiers.

## How to Beat Stall Teams
Stall teams rely on passive damage (Toxic, hazards), recovery moves and high defenses to wear you down. To beat them you need stallbreakers.
1. **Knock Off plus hazards**: removing Heavy-Duty Boots or Leftovers makes stall Pokemon vulnerable to Spikes and Stealth Rock.
2. **Taunt and Encore**: fast Taunt users (such as Heatran or Ogerpon) stop walls from using Recover or Toxic, and Encore locks them into passive moves.
3. **Setup sweepers**: Pokemon with Substitute or Stored Power can set up on passive walls.
4. **Wallbreakers**: big damage dealers with Choice Band or Choice Specs that 2HKO walls.
5. **Mixed attackers**: Pokemon that use both physical and special moves (like Iron Valiant) break specific defensive cores such as Blissey plus Dondozo.

## Hyper Offense (HO) Guide
Hyper Offense overwhelms the opponent quickly with high damage threats, trading defense for momentum.
1. **Suicide leads**: a dedicated lead (Glimmora, Deoxys-Speed, Ribombee) sets Stealth Rock, Spikes or Sticky Web and then faints so a sweeper enters safely.
2. **Screens**: Light Clay Alolan Ninetales or Grimmsnarl set Reflect and Light Screen so frail sweepers survive and set up.
3. **Setup sweepers**: the core of HO. Kingambit, Dragonite or Roaring Moon use Dragon Dance or Swords Dance to win in one turn.
4. **Spinblocking**: a Ghost type (Gholdengo) prevents the opponent from removing your hazards.

## Teambuilding Checklist
1. **Win condition**: how does this team win? For example, clear the checks to Kingambit so it can sweep.
2. **Speed control**: do you have a Choice Scarf user or priority moves (Sucker Punch, Extreme Speed) for fast threats?
3. **Hazard control**: can you set Stealth Rock, and can you remove hazards (Rapid Spin, Defog) or ignore them with Heavy-Duty Boots?
4. **Defensive core**: for balance and bulky offense, do you resist common attacking types like Ground, Fairy and Fire?
5. **Role compression**: fit several roles on one Pokemon. Great Tusk can be a spinner, a Ground check and a physical wall at once.

## Common Archetypes
1. **Balance**: a mix of defensive pivots and offensive threats that relies on safe switching.
2. **Bulky offense**: tanky attackers (Kingambit, Raging Bolt) that trade hits.
3. **Weather**: Sun (Torkoal with Protosynthesis users), Rain (Pelipper with Swift Swim users), Snow (Aurora Veil support).

## When to Terastallize
Each team can Terastallize one Pokemon once per battle, changing its type to its Tera type. Use it defensively to turn a weakness into a resistance at the moment that matters (Tera Fairy to stop a Dragon or Dark attack), or offensively to boost same type attacks and break a check. Saving Tera for the endgame usually wins more games than spending it on turn one, because the opponent has to respect it all game. Tera Blast becomes the Tera type, and Stellar Tera boosts every type once.

## Snow Teams
Snow Warning, Snowscape and Chilly Reception start snow for 5 turns, or 8 with Icy Rock. In Scarlet and Violet snow does no chip damage; instead it raises the Defense of Ice types by 50%. Slush Rush doubles Speed, Blizzard cannot miss, Weather Ball becomes an Ice move, and Aurora Veil, which halves both physical and special damage, can only be used in snow. Chilly Reception is also a pivot move, so a team can run it without building around snow.
